## Hotel data monetisation (`content/articles/hotel-data-monetisation.mdx`) — legal distinction needs correction

The sentence saying EU privacy regulation limits uses of personal data “even when anonymised” blurs anonymous and pseudonymised data. The European Commission and EDPB distinguish truly anonymous data from data that can identify someone again. This is a narrow claim check, not legal approval of the proposed business models or a check of California law.

Proposed replacement paragraph: “Before sharing guest-related datasets, establish whether individuals remain identifiable. Pseudonymised data remains personal data under the GDPR; genuinely anonymous data falls outside its scope. Aggregating or removing names does not by itself establish anonymity. Hotel teams should obtain qualified advice on re-identification risk, applicable law and the proposed use before entering a data-sharing arrangement.” Retain legal review as an operational action, not a blanket permission to sell guest data.

Evidence: [European Commission GDPR definitions](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/application-gdpr_en), [EDPB anonymisation and pseudonymisation](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/topics/ai-and-technology/anonymisation-pseudonymisation_en).
