Hyatt is changing the way it wants investors to evaluate its performance. Instead of focusing solely on the number of rooms, the company is emphasizing the importance of its premium guests. This shift in focus reflects the evolving nature of the hospitality industry, where luxury and high-end experiences are becoming increasingly valuable. 
   The move is significant, as it signals a change in the way Hyatt measures its success. By prioritizing premium guests, the company is acknowledging that the quality of its guests is more important than the quantity of its rooms. This approach is likely to have a positive impact on the company's revenue and profitability, as premium guests tend to spend more and generate higher margins. 
   However, Hyatt's growth pipeline is largely dependent on its Essentials brands, which are more focused on providing affordable and efficient accommodations. This presents a challenge for the company, as it must balance its desire to attract premium guests with the need to scale its more budget-friendly offerings. 
   The implications of this shift are far-reaching, and will likely be felt throughout the hospitality industry. As other companies take note of Hyatt's approach, they may begin to re-evaluate their own priorities and focus on attracting higher-end guests. This could lead to a shift towards more luxury-focused development, as companies seek to capitalize on the growing demand for high-end experiences. 
   For hotel owners and operators, this change in focus presents both opportunities and challenges. On the one hand, it highlights the importance of investing in premium amenities and services that will attract high-end guests. On the other hand, it requires a careful balancing act between luxury and affordability, as companies must ensure that they are meeting the needs of a diverse range of travelers. 
   Ultimately, Hyatt's decision to prioritize premium guests reflects a deeper understanding of the hospitality industry and its evolving needs. As the company continues to navigate this shift, it will be important to monitor its progress and assess the impact on its bottom line. 
   The success of this strategy will depend on Hyatt's ability to effectively communicate its value proposition to investors and guests alike. By emphasizing the quality of its guests and the luxury of its experiences, the company can differentiate itself in a crowded market and drive long-term growth and profitability.
