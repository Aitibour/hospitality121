A supplier case study about Williamsburg Lodge describes a shift from explaining upgrades mainly at reception to presenting options digitally before arrival. Canary Technologies says its Dynamic Upsells product lets guests request room upgrades and additional services, with hotel staff approving requests.

The published revenue figures are supplier-reported customer estimates. They are not independently audited here, and a change from annual estimates to monthly averages does not provide a like-for-like comparison on its own.

## An offer needs an operating plan

Hospitality121's analysis: a digital menu is only useful when the property can fulfil what it sells. An early arrival offer, for example, requires a clear view of room readiness and an agreed response when availability changes.

The same applies to connecting rooms or a requested amenity. Before making an offer visible, a team can identify who confirms it, how it reaches the relevant department and how the guest is told whether it has been accepted.

## Measure what the hotel keeps

A property evaluating its own programme could record the number of offers viewed, requests accepted, cancellations, delivery problems and staff time needed to fulfil an order. Added revenue alone does not describe the cost of the work or its effect on the guest experience.

These are proposed evaluation measures, not results from Williamsburg Lodge. The case study illustrates a reported workflow change, rather than establishing a return that another hotel should expect.

A useful first experiment is one offer with a reliable fulfilment process. Expand only when the team can explain both the commercial result and the service workload.
