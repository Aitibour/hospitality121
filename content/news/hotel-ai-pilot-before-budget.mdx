An AI budget line is easy to add. Deciding what a hotel should test first takes a more specific conversation: which job is repetitive, what does it cost the team today and how would an improvement be recognised?

Otel's announcement for its 2027 hotel AI guide, published on its own website on 22 October and covered by Hotel Technology News on 27 October, proposes selecting one use case and testing it alongside the existing process over four weeks. The guide includes ten templates. Those are features of the published guidance, not evidence that every hotel can complete a successful implementation within that period.

## Make the decision smaller

Hospitality121's analysis: a first test becomes easier to judge when the team can describe its boundaries in a sentence. For example, a property could evaluate draft answers to a defined set of routine guest questions while a colleague continues to approve every response. The objective would be to compare a specific piece of work, rather than hand an entire department to a new system.

Before the trial, record how the task is currently completed. Useful observations might include the time needed to prepare a response, how often it requires correction and which questions are escalated. These are suggested measures, not benchmarks or promised savings.

## Record the inconvenient cases

A convincing demonstration can hide the exceptions that make hotel work difficult. Include an outdated room policy, a question with missing information and a request that should be passed to a person. Keep a record of corrections as well as successful answers.

Agree who can pause the trial and how the team returns to its normal process. The ability to stop is part of a useful experiment, not a sign that the experiment has failed.

## Give the result somewhere to go

At the end, management needs a decision: continue within the same limits, adjust the test, stop it or consider a wider rollout. A short evaluation can justify a next step without proving that a property is ready for broad automation. More complex integrations may require a longer assessment.
