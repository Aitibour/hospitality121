UrVenue announced an expanded Stripe integration on November 11 for payments tied to resort and hospitality experiences. The upgraded checkout continues to accept credit and debit cards and adds Amazon Pay, Apple Pay, Cash App Pay, Google Pay and Link, according to the company.

UrVenue also says it has introduced Stripe Adaptive Pricing, which can display prices and allow eligible guests to pay in their local currency. The announcement notes that payment-method availability depends on location, currency, device and account configuration.

The integration applies to bookable experiences such as pools, cabanas, dining, nightlife, activities and entertainment. It should not be read as evidence that every UrVenue customer has enabled every wallet or currency option.

## Test the full payment path

Hospitality121 analysis: operators should verify more than whether a wallet button appears. A rollout test should cover the displayed price, currency conversion disclosure, taxes, cancellation terms, confirmation message and refund path for each enabled market.

The reconciliation process matters as much as checkout. Finance teams need to know how an experience purchase appears in settlement reports, how a refund is matched to the original booking, and whether chargeback evidence includes the relevant reservation and policy details.

## A focused pre-launch checklist

- Confirm which wallets appear on supported devices and browsers.
- Compare the guest's displayed currency with the settlement currency.
- Test cancellation and refund messages for an experience booking.
- Make ownership clear when a payment succeeds but inventory confirmation fails.

These are operational recommendations, not reported UrVenue customer outcomes. The announcement describes added payment options and eligibility conditions, while each resort controls its own configuration and operating process.
